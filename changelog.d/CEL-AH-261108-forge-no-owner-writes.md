### Changed
- `cel forge pr close`, `pr ready` and `run rerun` have no owner path any more. A write needs a positively identified product agent: `CEL_ROLE` solo or orchestrator AND a product (`CEL_PRODUCT` and/or its `<product>-orch` mailbox, agreeing when both are set) that owns the repo, on a PR its workspace account authored or a migrated Forgejo PR (author `Ghost`). Anything missing or contradictory is refused, and a process with no agent identity is told to use the forge's web UI (Forgejo or GitHub) or `gh` directly. An agent could previously pass as the owner by shedding its marks (`setsid -f env -u CEL_ROLE cel forge ...`, `env -S '-u CEL_ROLE'`, `bash -c 'unset CEL_ROLE; ...'`); with nothing to gain from that, the bypass is gone. Read verbs (`repo`, `whoami`, `pr view|list|checks|reviews`, `run list|view|logs`) stay open to everyone.
- The guard no longer refuses `cel forge` lines that only unset `CEL_*` variables (`unset`, `env -u`, `env -i`): `cel forge` itself refuses the identity-less result. Lines that set a `CEL_*` variable (even to empty) are still refused, as is sourcing `lib/forge.sh` or calling its functions.
- `cel team` has no owner path either. `start`, `add`, `retire`, `close` and `cap` need the positively identified lead: `CEL_ROLE` solo or orchestrator and a product (`CEL_PRODUCT` and/or the `<product>-orch` mailbox, agreeing) naming the team's product. A `decision` event (#261 allowed lead or owner) and `candidate.updated` are the lead's only, and a verdict is a member's or a reviewer's. A process with no agent role - including an agent after `env -u CEL_ROLE` or `setsid` - is refused, and told the owner asks the product's agent by `cel inbox` or `cel decide`. Members keep `ack`, `event` and `verdict` by their member identity; `status` stays open.
- The guard's environment-tampering refusal covers every `cel` command, not only `cel forge`: a line that runs `cel` while setting, emptying, exporting or declaring any `CEL_*` or `HERDR_*` variable is refused for every agent role (`CEL_GUARD_ALLOW_ONCE` is not counted; `cel inbox send --from` is a flag). Previously `CEL_PRODUCT=other CEL_INBOX_ME=other-orch cel team start ...` let one product's agent lead another product's team.
- `cel team`'s writing verbs check the caller's identity against its launch: the nearest process above `cel` whose `/proc/<pid>/environ` (fixed at exec) carries `CEL_ROLE` must agree on `CEL_ROLE`, `CEL_PRODUCT`, `CEL_INBOX_ME`, `CEL_INBOX_WS` and `CEL_TEAM*`, and a role with no launch above it (detached by `setsid` or a double fork) is refused. A process that execs a fresh environment carrying `CEL_ROLE` is its own nearest launch; that spelling stays the guard's to refuse.
